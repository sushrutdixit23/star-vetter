## Eccentric orbits

A displaced secondary eclipse gives e cos(omega) = (pi/2)(phase - 0.5), a lower limit on the eccentricity. A star is listed only when two independent halves of the TESS data each show the dip at the same phase, no third dip exists, and no fast periodic signal is present outside the eclipses.

## Tidal circularisation check

Close binaries are expected to be circular. Offsets of |e cos w| of at least 0.05: 0 of 32 measured systems at P of 2 d or less, 10 of 74 above 2 d.
If the short systems had the same rate as the longer ones, the chance of seeing none is 0.0096. This reproduces known physics; it validates the measurement and is not a new result.

## Timing drift (third-body search)

Eclipse times per TESS sector and per ZTF season come from one shared template. Counts: CONSTANT 60, DRIFT_CONFIRMED 2, DRIFT_TESS_ONLY 1, DRIFT_TOO_FEW_TIMES 10, DRIFT_UNCONFIRMED 1, IMPLAUSIBLE_AMPLITUDE 29, NOT_TESTABLE 203, PERIOD_CHANGE_SMOOTH 1.
Many raw drift flags are measurement failures (template mismatch or cycle-count slips) and are rejected by amplitude, minimum-times and smoothness rules; only a parabola that fits well and has both instruments on one curve is kept.
False-alarm rate of the full rule on 141 synthetic constant-period light curves (real sampling and noise): 0 flagged.

The cause is not settled (a distant third body, mass exchange and others all produce a parabola over a few years). Predictions of the next eclipses are in `docs/period_change_predictions.json`.

Light-travel-time test for TIC 237115819 (circular third-body orbit, 2 extra parameters over the parabola): best fit P3 = 8.0 yr with chi2 6.9 against 9.8 for the parabola, so it improves on (not significantly, for two extra parameters) the parabola. Orbits with P3 from 5.9 to 400.0 yr are statistically allowed; capping the unseen companion at 1.5 Msun leaves 5.9 to 22.3 yr, light-time amplitude 9 to 55 min, minimum companion mass 0.37 to 1.75 Msun. Orbits shorter than about 5.9 yr are excluded by the data. This does not show a third body exists.

## External check of the eccentricity measurement

The same code was run on 50 TESS eclipsing binaries from the Prsa et al. 2022 catalog (published secondary phases, fitted by other code), using only the published period and epoch as input and the first 26 sectors the catalog was fitted on.
Measured secondary phase against the published phase: median absolute difference 0.0003, 100% of measured systems within 0.01 in phase.
Published as circular: 27 systems, 0 called eccentric (false positives). Published as eccentric: 23 systems, 18 recovered; the rest were vetoed or lacked enough secondary eclipses to test, which is by design (the rule prefers a miss to a false claim).
Verdicts for published-eccentric systems: ECCENTRIC 18, INSUFFICIENT_FOR_HALVES 5.

## Limits

- Only e cos(omega) is measured, so each value is a lower limit on the eccentricity.
- Eclipses are on the TESS aperture, not proven to be on the target (see the evidence tier).
- Selection: a secondary must be detectable, which favours deep, well-separated eclipses.
